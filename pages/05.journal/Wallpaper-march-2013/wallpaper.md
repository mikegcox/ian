        comment: 'The March 2013 Wallpaper shows the musical ensemble west bound about to pass to the north of the green navigational light at the Punts Islands.  The Punts, named by island surveyor Owen in 1816, include the main island and Isles 34b, c, d, e, and f. The large land mass in the background is Thwartway Island (also known locally as Leek Island), part of St. Lawrence Islands National Park.  (The National Park is about to go through a name change after 109 years - to become "Thousand Islands National Park").  As described by Susie Smith in her book "The First Summer People", punts were small utility boats used during the early island surveys which would be used by the crew in shallow water for rowing between ships.  She also notes that the 0.8 acre main island was originally valued at $10 in 1873 by island surveyor Unwin and was later sold in 1904 for $220.  This particular green flashing navigation light is an important dog''s leg turn in the eastern end of the Leek Island Channel, a segment of the Canadian Middle Channel.'

My grandparents purchased the island before there was even a house on it, and spent many summers camping with my dad & aunts on a tent platform to the left of that dock. I thought I&rsquo;d share a somewhat different image this month, not just to revisit summer, but to celebrate the fact that <a href="http://www.greatlakeswimmers.com">Great lake Swimmers' </a>latest album, <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Wild_Everywhere"><em>New Wild Everywhere</em></a>, was just nominated for a Juno.&nbsp; For those who may not know, the Junos are Canada&rsquo;s top music awards, our equivalent to the Grammies and this is the second time the Swimmers have been so honored.</p>
<p><span style="font-size: 10pt; font-style: normal; font-family: Verdana, Helvetica;">If you don't yet know them, </span><a href="http://www.1000islandsphotoart.com/LinkClick.aspx?link=http%3a%2f%2fwww.greatlakeswimmers.com%2f&amp;tabid=197&amp;portalid=1&amp;mid=618" style="font-family: Verdana, Helvetica; font-size: 10pt; font-style: normal;">Great Lake Swimmers</a><span style="font-size: 10pt; font-style: normal; font-family: Verdana, Helvetica;"> are an acclaimed band that CBC has termed as &ldquo;Canada&rsquo;s national treasure,&rdquo; who have aligned themselves very closely with the 1000 Islands region.&nbsp;</span><br>
<br>
This all began when they recorded tracks at Singer Castle, the <a href="http://www.brockvilleartscentre.com">Brockville Arts Centre</a> and Rockport&rsquo;s St. Brendan&rsquo;s Church in 2008. Just as I had been when I stumbled across the 1000 Islands by accident, the band&rsquo;s leader/singer/songwriter Tony Dekker was blown away by what he found here. He titled that album <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Channels"><em>Lost Channels</em></a> and then spent two years singing and talking about the River in concerts and interviews around the world, as far away as China.<br>
<br>
This was followed in 2009 by a GLS concert on the deck of the Tall Ship <a href="http://www.tallshipsadventure.org/"><em>Fair Jeanne</em></a><em></em> in Brockville&rsquo;s harbor, an event staged by <a href="http://www.tallshipslanding.com">Tall Ships Landing</a>, a major, multi-faceted project, which at that point was still in its formative stages. The music video <a href="http://www.1000islandsphotoart.com/Media/GreatLakeSwimmersPalmistry.aspx"><em>Palmistry</em></a> came next, filmed aboard the <em>Fair Jeanne</em> under sail, at <a href="http://www.singercastle.com/">Singer Castle</a> and on Raleigh Island. Another music video from that album, <a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgsspjVOJBw"><em>River&rsquo;s Edge</em></a><em></em>, was released in &rsquo;09.<br>
<br>
In 2010, the Swimmers sold out 3 concerts here, including at the Brockville Arts Centre and the <a href="http://claytonoperahouse.com">Clayton Opera House</a> (both of which ran in conjunction with the launch of my final printed book, &ldquo;<a href="http://www.1000islandsphotoart.com/tabid/423/articleType/ArticleView/articleId/57/The-very-best-of-Ian-Coristines-1000-Islands-V.aspx"><em>The very best of Ian Coristine's 1000 Islands</em></a>&rdquo;) and a third concert to support <a href="http://www.waterkeeper.ca">Lake Ontario&rsquo;s Waterkeeper</a> on Wolfe Island.<br>
<br>
In 2012, my sixth book, <a href="http://www.oneinathousand.ca"><em>One in a Thousand</em></a> was released as <a href="https://itunes.apple.com/us/app/one-in-a-thousand/id518088485?ls=1&amp;mt=8">an interactive iPad app</a>. It is a true story, co-written by the astonishingly talented Donna Walsh Inglehart, that chronicles my discovery of the region and many subsequent adventures here, similar in theme to <em>A Year in Provence</em>, <em>Under the Tuscan Sun</em> or <em>The Olive Farm</em>.<br>
<br>
Thanks to a wonderful team of creative tech geniuses, the <a href="http://www.mclellangroup.com">McLellan Group</a> re-imagined the future of publishing by bringing the story to life in ways not previously possible, as an iPad App. It is told with an 86,000 word text, illustrated with over 450 images, numerous videos, nature sounds, narration, an interactive map and very significantly, 18 unpublished instrumental versions of Great Lake Swimmers&rsquo; hits. <br>
<br>
Early last summer, Tony performed solo at Kim Lunman&rsquo;s <a href="http://www.islandlifemag.ca/">Island Life Magazine</a> launch party for <a href="http://www.oneinathousand.ca"><em>One in a Thousand</em></a> at <a href="http://www.ivylea.ca/Restaurant/tabid/458/Default.aspx">the Ivy</a> and later, Tony brought the band back for McLellan Group to shoot an outstanding music video, <a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yC7FAHZGQ8o"><em>Ballad of a Fisherman&rsquo;s Wife</em></a> at various locations, including aboard this funky barge which served as the stage for what must be one of the River&rsquo;s most unique ever concerts. The only fans in attendance were the film crew and the loons until a Gananoque tour boat spotted us and provided an impromtu and very enthusiastic triple-deck audience! You&rsquo;ll find more behind the scenes images from this magical day on <a href="http://pinterest.com/iancoristine/thousand-islands/">my Pinterest page</a>.<br>
<br>
This summer, the Swimmers will return on June 14th for a major River event that should definitely go on your calendar now. An elaborate War of 1812 <a href="http://celebrate1812.ca/events/tallships">tall ships festival</a> in Brockville (featuring 13 tall ships) will serve as the backdrop to the building dedication ceremony for <a href="http://www.tallshipslanding.com">Tall Ships Landing</a> and (coming in 2014) the 1000 Islands' own discovery center - <a href="http://www.aquatarium.ca">Aquatarium</a>, which is the Genesis of a hugely important initiative - <a href="http://www.1000islandsriverquest.com">RiverQuest</a>, that is uniting the entire region to present it to the world like another Tuscany or Provence.<br>
<br>
The stage and setting for the Swimmers' (free) concert will again be the <em>Fair Jeanne</em>, this time lit up under the stars in Brockville&rsquo;s harbor. If weather requires, the show will move to the nearby <a href="http://www.brockvilleartscentre.com/">Brockville Arts Centre</a>. This will be a truly magical evening.&nbsp;</p>
