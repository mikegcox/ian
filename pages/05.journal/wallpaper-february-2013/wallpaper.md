 <p>I suspect that many of you have had limited exposure to the River during the off season. For that reason and because this is my first full winter here, I tend to think it&rsquo;s more interesting to continue to share winter moods than reverting to summer, at least for the moment. If you&rsquo;re starved for summer scenes, there are plenty archived in earlier wallpapers or in the dozens of <a href="http://www.1000islandsphotoart.com/Albums.aspx">Albums</a> you&rsquo;ll find elsewhere on the site under Books, Places and Themes.</p>
<p>Speaking of which, perhaps I should take the opportunity to plug our iPad App <em><a href="https://itunes.apple.com/us/app/one-in-a-thousand/id518088485?ls=1&amp;mt=8">One in a Thousand</a></em>, which has plenty more. <a href="http://http://www.oneinathousand.ca/"><em>One</em></a>, produced by the astonishingly talented team at <a href="http://www.mclellangroup.com/mip">McLellan Interactive Publishing,</a> recently won two <a href="http://blog.amcpros.com/2012/09/14/marcom-winner-mclellan-multimedia/">MarCom platinum awards</a> for excellence in communications. It is a love story about the River, costs $9 and in addition to the 85,000 word memoir, includes a number of videos, River sounds, an interactive map, 18 unpublished Great Lake Swimmers instrumental tracks, 2 music videos and 455 of my images, many of which have not been seen elsewhere.</p>
<p>On the River the temperatures this winter have been going up and down like a yo-yo. We recently emerged from a bitterly cold spell which lasted about a week, and during the last couple of days the temperature reached 75 degrees higher than a week or so ago!</p>
<p>For this month, I was tempted to share a pretty sunrise image taken during the deep freeze with sea smoke billowing in the background, but instead decided on this image captured during a thaw which struck me as a little more out of the ordinary. I have a portrait version which I prefer, but as it won&rsquo;t satisfy the landscape proportions of a monitor, I&rsquo;ll go with this one for this month's wallpaper. You can see the portrait version on my <a href="http://pinterest.com/pin/133630313915421814/">Pinterest</a> page. Please don&rsquo;t be shy to share any stories this image may prompt about winter or fog that you may have experienced.</p>
<p>While we endure the long winter lull, all is not as quiet as you may think on the River. Pivotal things are going on. I&rsquo;d like to spotlight a very important article written by Susie Smith in the current issue of <a href="http://www.thousandislandslife.com">TI Life Magazine</a>.</p>
<p>TI Life&rsquo;s founder, Paul Malo and I often discussed how the Thousand Islands could make a far greater impact were it to present itself to the world as a unique and united binational whole rather than having the countries, towns and attractions all presenting their messages individually. Working together as a cohesive entity, this region is no less worthy than, and can become another important international tourism Mecca like France's <a href="http://www.lonelyplanet.com/france/provence">Provence</a> or Italy's <a href="http://www.lonelyplanet.com/italy/tuscany">Tuscany</a> or <a href="http://www.lonelyplanet.com/italy/cinque-terre"></a><a href="http://www.lonelyplanet.com/italy/cinque-terre">Cinque Terra</a>.</p>
<p>A critically important initiative known as <a href="http://www.1000islandsriverquest.com">RiverQuest</a> is underway to accomplish precisely this. You can learn more about it from Susie&rsquo;s (updated) article, <a href="http://www.thousandislandslife.com/BackIssues/Archive/tabid/393/articleType/ArticleView/articleId/1139/RiverQuest-Discovered.aspx">RiverQuest Discovered</a> in the January edition of TI Life Magazine.<br>
