<p>Several people came close to identifying last month’s wallpaper image location, pointing to various spots in and around the Admiralties, but only one, Kim Yetto, picked the right island. When British surveyor Charles Unwin set out on the monumental task of surveying and naming all the islands in 1873, he began right here at Island Number 1, Windward. If you look closely, you’ll also see Windward (the smaller of the two) dead centre in this month’s image.</p> 

<p><img alt="Wallpaper April 2015" src="http://www.1000islandsphotoart.com/Portals/ian/wallpapers/1000_islands_wallpaper_April_2015_small.jpg" style="width: 400px; height: 225px;"></p> <p>Many years ago, I took a shot from this perspective on another amazingly clear evening. It was on film and not taken with a particularly good camera, but it became the first image I ever published (as a poster) in 1999. I always hoped to capture this view again with a really good camera, because it says “1000 Islands” better than any other that I've taken. However, crystal clear days when you can see the River disappearing over the horizon happen once in a blue moon and you need amazingly good luck to be in the air at the right place at the right time. It took 16 years for me to get lucky again, this time with highest resolution camera I’ve ever owned which reveals detail that would never have been possible before.</p> <p>The features of all of the Admiralties are clearly visible as well as most of the Lake Fleet and Navy Group Islands. Check out the 1000 Islands Tower and both the Canadian and U.S. Spans in the distance and where the River makes its northerly jog at Rockport. I could never have dreamt of images like this when I began.</p>

<p>It’s pictures like this that are why I felt compelled to do another book after I had clearly stated that “<a href="http://bit.ly/1fX14gs">The very best of…</a>" would be the last. It would be a crime not to share them with others who are passionate about the place. This time though, this really is my last book. Why? Because over the years, inflation has crept in, making it almost impossible to print a book with the physical quality that I insist on, at an affordable price.</p> <p>There is only one way to make sense of it. Roll the dice and take a big gamble on a truly enormous print run to get the unit price down from what would normally be at least $60 to a level the casual tourist is likely to find appealing. There simply aren’t enough River people to make this work, but hopefully, over several years, there will be enough tourists. You may not have realized it, but the last book was extremely good value at $40 U.S. because it’s the only book ever printed with (expensive and technically very difficult) cover lamination on every page.</p>

It’s very seldom that you’ll find a regional book of this physical quality at any price, and certainly never at this price.
